POSS vs. CAST: Which EEI Exam Do You Need?

If a utility job posting mentions an "EEI test" without saying which one, there's a good chance it means either POSS or CAST — two completely different Edison Electric Institute exam batteries that get confused constantly. One screens candidates for power plant operator roles. The other screens candidates for line worker, electrician, and other construction and skilled-trades roles. Here's exactly how they differ, and how to tell which one you're actually being asked to take.

Two different jobs, two different tests

Both POSS and CAST are developed and validated by the Edison Electric Institute (EEI), the trade association of investor-owned electric utility companies. CertPath Publishing is an independent publisher and is not affiliated with or endorsed by EEI. Beyond that shared origin, the two tests exist for different hiring pipelines:

  • POSS (Plant Operator Selection System) is used to screen candidates for power plant operator positions — the people who monitor and control equipment inside fossil, nuclear, or hydro generating stations.
  • CAST (Construction and Skilled Trades Selection System) is used to screen candidates for line worker, electrician, and other construction and skilled-trades apprentice positions — the people who build and maintain the poles, lines, and substations that move power from the plant to the customer.

If your job posting names the role rather than the test, that's usually the fastest way to tell which one you'll sit: control-room and plant-operator postings point to POSS, and line-crew or trades-apprentice postings point to CAST.

What's on the CAST test

CAST has 110 scored questions split across four separately timed sections, for a total of 87 minutes of timed testing. No calculator is allowed on any section:

  • Graphic Arithmetic — 16 questions, 30 minutes. Reading dimensions and quantities off a print or drawing and doing the math.
  • Mathematical Usage — 18 questions, 7 minutes. Word problems with basic arithmetic, ratios and rates — the tightest time limit on the test.
  • Mechanical Concepts — 44 questions, 20 minutes. Gears, levers, pulleys, and basic physical reasoning.
  • Reading for Comprehension — 32 questions, 30 minutes. Applying a written rule or procedure to a specific scenario.

What's on the POSS test

POSS has 146 scored questions split across four separately timed sections, for a total of 77 minutes of timed testing. Like CAST, no calculator is allowed on any section:

  • Figural Reasoning — 20 questions, 10 minutes. Spotting the pattern in a series of shapes or progressions — a section CAST doesn't have.
  • Mathematical Usage — 46 questions, 17 minutes. Word problems with arithmetic, ratios and rates, similar in style to CAST's math section but with more than twice the questions.
  • Mechanical Concepts — 44 questions, 20 minutes. Gears, levers, pulleys, and basic physical reasoning — the same section, timing and question count as CAST.
  • Reading for Comprehension — 36 questions, 30 minutes. Applying a written rule or procedure to a specific scenario.

The four timed POSS sections add up to 77 minutes, but the full testing appointment — including check-in, instructions, practice items and breaks — typically runs closer to two hours, the same pattern CAST follows at 87 minutes of timed sections plus administrative time on top.

A closer look: why the math sections feel so rushed

Both tests give you well under 30 seconds per question on their Mathematical Usage sections — about 23 seconds each on CAST's 18 questions in 7 minutes, and about 22 seconds each on POSS's 46 questions in 17 minutes. Neither section allows a calculator, which means the real skill being tested isn't arithmetic ability, it's recognizing the shortest path to an answer under time pressure. Here's the kind of two-step rate problem both sections favor (this is an original illustrative example, not an actual exam question):

A tank's pressure gauge reads 92 psi. After a 2-minute startup delay, pressure rises at 3.5 psi per minute. A relief valve opens at 145 psi. How many minutes from now does the valve open?

Work it in two steps. First, find how much pressure still has to build: 145 − 92 = 53 psi. Then divide by the rate to find how long the rise itself takes: 53 ÷ 3.5 ≈ 15.1 minutes. Add back the 2-minute delay that happened before the rise even started: 15.1 + 2 ≈ 17 minutes. The most common trap on a problem like this is dropping the startup delay and answering with just the 15.1 minutes — which is why both CAST and POSS math sections reward reading the full problem before doing any arithmetic, not after.

How the tests are scored

Neither test gives you a simple pass/fail line. Both combine performance across all sections into a single index rather than reporting a per-section score, and EEI doesn't publish one universal passing number — each hiring utility sets its own minimum for the role it's filling. That also means there's no fixed public registration fee the way there is for a credentialing exam like the CSP: POSS and CAST are administered by the individual utility as part of its own hiring process, not scheduled directly through EEI, so the exact process, cost (if any), and retake window depend entirely on the employer you're applying to.

Which one do you actually need? A quick checklist

  • Applying for line worker, apprentice lineman, electrician, or another construction/skilled-trades craft role? You need CAST.
  • Applying for power plant operator, control room operator, or auxiliary operator at a fossil, nuclear, or hydro plant? You need POSS.
  • Job posting just says "EEI test" without naming one? Ask the recruiter which battery — the two tests share a format but not a syllabus, and studying the wrong one wastes real time.
  • Recruiter mentions MASS or TECH instead? Those are separate EEI batteries for maintenance and technical roles, not covered by either guide above — confirm the exact test name before you start studying.

If you're not sure which role a posting maps to, the job title is usually the clearest signal: anything with "operator" in the title at a generating station points to POSS, and anything with "lineman," "electrician," "apprentice," or "technician" tied to construction or line work points to CAST.

Can you be asked to take both?

Yes — some candidates who apply to more than one role at the same utility, or to utilities that combine hiring pipelines, may be asked to sit both tests at different points in the process. Since scheduling, fees, and retake windows are all set by the individual employer rather than by EEI directly, the only reliable way to know your specific timeline is to ask the utility's HR or recruiting contact rather than assume a policy that applies elsewhere.

What's the difference between the POSS and CAST tests?

POSS screens candidates for power plant operator roles (fossil, nuclear or hydro plants). CAST screens candidates for line worker, electrician and other construction and skilled-trades roles. POSS includes a Figural Reasoning section that CAST doesn't have, and CAST includes a Graphic Arithmetic section that POSS doesn't have.

Can I use a calculator on the POSS or CAST test?

No. EEI prohibits calculators on every section of both the POSS and CAST tests, along with its other technical and maintenance batteries.

Who administers POSS and CAST, and how do I register?

Both are administered by the individual utility as part of its own hiring process, not scheduled directly through EEI, so exact registration steps, fees and retake windows depend on the employer you're applying to.

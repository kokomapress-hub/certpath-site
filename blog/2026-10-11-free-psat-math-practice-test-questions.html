4 PSAT/NMSQT Math questions, worked step by step

These four come from CertPath's free 25-question PSAT/NMSQT Math sample set and are quoted exactly as written there, one from each of the section's four content domains.

Question 1 — Algebra: renting an aerator under a budget

A crew rents an aerator for a $65.00 delivery charge plus $22.50 for each hour the machine is kept, and the fuel it burns costs an additional $4.75 for each of those hours. A 7% sales tax is applied to the total of all of these charges. If the crew has $400.00 to spend, what is the greatest whole number of hours it can keep the aerator?

  • A. 11
  • B. 13
  • C. 12
  • D. 14

Answer: A. Each hour costs 22.50 + 4.75 = $27.25, so the pre-tax total is 65 + 27.25h and the taxed total is 1.07(65 + 27.25h), which must be at most $400.00. That gives 27.25h ≤ 308.83, so h ≤ 11.3, and the greatest whole number of hours is 11, costing $390.28.

Skill tested and common trap: this checks whether you apply tax to the whole bill, including the fixed delivery charge, before testing a budget constraint. Choice C, 12 hours, drops the 7% tax entirely and solves (400 − 65) ÷ 27.25 = 12.3, which would spend $392.50 without tax but $419.98 once the real 7% is added.

Question 2 — Advanced Math: when a quadratic has two real solutions

In the equation 3x² + kx + 27 = 0, k is a constant. For which values of k does the equation have two distinct real solutions?

  • A. k < -9 or k > 9
  • B. k > 18
  • C. -18 < k < 18
  • D. k < -18 or k > 18

Answer: D. Two distinct real solutions require a positive discriminant: k² − 4(3)(27) = k² − 324 > 0, so k² > 324 and k must be beyond 18 in either direction.

Skill tested and common trap: this checks whether you remember the full discriminant formula b² − 4ac rather than a shortcut version of it. Choice A, beyond 9 in either direction, drops the factor of 4 from 4ac and uses the square root of 3 × 27 = 81 as the cutoff instead of the square root of 4 × 3 × 27 = 324.

Question 3 — Problem-Solving and Data Analysis: a weighted average backward

A course has two sections. Section 1 has 18 students whose mean score on an exam was 76. Section 2 has 12 students. The instructor wants the mean score of all 30 students to be at least 82. What is the least mean score Section 2 can have for that to happen?

  • A. 91
  • B. 88
  • C. 82
  • D. 86

Answer: A. All 30 students must total at least 30(82) = 2,460 points. Section 1 already supplies 18(76) = 1,368 points, so Section 2's 12 students must total at least 1,092 points, a mean of at least 91.

Skill tested and common trap: this checks whether you weight each section's mean by how many students are actually in it. Choice B, 88, comes from solving (76 + x) ÷ 2 = 82, treating the two sections as if they held an equal number of students, which understates what the smaller section actually has to do.

Question 4 — Geometry and Trigonometry: height of a tower from eye level

A surveyor stands on level ground 84 feet from the base of a tower. From the surveyor's eye, which is 5.5 feet above the ground, the angle of elevation to the top of the tower measures 38°. To the nearest foot, what is the height of the tower?

  • A. 66 feet
  • B. 57 feet
  • C. 113 feet
  • D. 71 feet

Answer: D. The horizontal distance of 84 feet is adjacent to the 38° angle, and the rise above eye level is opposite it, so that rise is 84 × tan(38°) ≈ 65.6 feet. The tower's full height also includes the 5.5 feet from the ground up to the surveyor's eye, so the height is about 65.6 + 5.5 = 71 feet.

Skill tested and common trap: this checks whether you account for the surveyor's eye height instead of measuring from the ground. Choice A, 66 feet, stops at the rise above eye level and leaves out the 5.5 feet below the line of sight entirely.

PSAT/NMSQT Math format: what you'll actually sit down to take

Per College Board's own description of the digital SAT Suite, the PSAT/NMSQT Math section has 44 questions split into two modules of 22 questions each, 35 minutes per module, for 70 minutes total. The section is adaptive: how you perform on the first module decides whether the second module skews easier or harder, which is how the test narrows in on your actual level. A built-in graphing calculator is available for the entire section — there's no calculator-free portion. Math sits inside a full test that runs 2 hours and 14 minutes altogether, preceded by a 54-question, 64-minute Reading and Writing section (also split into two modules) and a 10-minute break before Math begins. See College Board's own Digital PSAT/NMSQT student guide for the full structure.

What the Math section actually covers: four domains

Both the PSAT/NMSQT and the Digital SAT draw their math questions from the same four content domains, but they don't weight them identically. Per College Board's published content domain breakdown, PSAT/NMSQT Math weighs roughly:

  • Algebra — about 35%. Linear equations and systems, linear inequalities, and interpreting the parts of a linear model (Question 1 above).
  • Advanced Math — about 32.5%. Quadratic and other nonlinear equations, functions, and manipulating equivalent algebraic expressions (Question 2 above).
  • Problem-Solving and Data Analysis — about 20%. Ratios, rates, percentages, and reading or reasoning from tables, scatterplots and two-way data, including weighted averages (Question 3 above).
  • Geometry and Trigonometry — about 12.5%. Area, volume, lines, angles, triangles, and right-triangle trigonometry (Question 4 above).

That's a slightly different mix than the SAT, which puts a bit more weight on Advanced Math and Geometry and Trigonometry and a bit less on Problem-Solving and Data Analysis. The practical effect on a 44-question Math section is small — roughly one question's worth of shift per domain — but it does mean PSAT/NMSQT test-takers see marginally more data-and-rates questions and marginally fewer geometry questions than their SAT-taking peers.

A worked example: budgeting study time against the real weights

Say you can commit 20 total hours to PSAT/NMSQT Math review before test day. Weighted to the published domain percentages instead of split evenly across four topics, that time should break down roughly like this:

  • Algebra (35%): 20 × 0.35 = 7 hours
  • Advanced Math (32.5%): 20 × 0.325 = 6.5 hours
  • Problem-Solving and Data Analysis (20%): 20 × 0.20 = 4 hours
  • Geometry and Trigonometry (12.5%): 20 × 0.125 = 2.5 hours

That's 7 + 6.5 + 4 + 2.5 = 20 hours, weighted toward Algebra and Advanced Math — together two-thirds of the section — instead of giving each of the four domains an equal five hours.

How it's scored

Math is scored on a 160-to-760 scale, the same range used for the Reading and Writing section, and the two section scores add together for a total between 320 and 1520, per College Board's own scoring pages. That's a lower ceiling than the Digital SAT's 200–800-per-section, 400–1600-total scale — not a harder curve, just a different scale built for students who are typically a year or more away from the content depth the full SAT scale spreads out.

Who takes it, and why it matters for National Merit

The PSAT/NMSQT is given at school, usually during a single fall testing window each district schedules for its own students, and most schools order it for every 11th grader. The junior-year sitting is the one that counts: it's the qualifying test for the National Merit Scholarship Program, run separately by the National Merit Scholarship Corporation. Scores from the PSAT 10 or PSAT 8/9, taken in earlier grades, share the same score scale but are not used for National Merit entry — only the junior-year PSAT/NMSQT counts for that program.

What it costs

College Board's list price for the PSAT/NMSQT is about $18 per student. In practice, most schools order the test for their juniors as a group and cover some or all of that cost themselves rather than billing families directly, and fee waivers are available for students with financial need. Because the exact amount you're asked to pay — if any — depends on how your school or district handles ordering, check with your school's counseling office rather than assuming the list price applies to you.
